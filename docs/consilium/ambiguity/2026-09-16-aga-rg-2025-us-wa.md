# Guidance ambiguity review — Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide 2025 — Washington

> Source: https://www.americangaming.org/resources/responsible-gaming-regulations-and-statutes-guide/ (agency_guidance, US-WA, effective None). Scanned 2026-09-16: 118 textual findings; 9 judged material by the Consilium (claude-fable-5-1). Clarification request below is a DRAFT — NOT SENT; a named human must approve any submission.

AGA's 2025 Washington chapter compiles (not authors) the state's responsible-gaming law: RCW 9.46.0364 (tribal sports wagering compact amendments must "address" responsible and problem gambling), RCW 9.46.071 (statewide self-exclusion mandate, forfeiture, civil immunity, data-use limit, fee-increase bar keyed to RCW 82.04.285(2)) and WAC 230-23-001 through -020 (enrollment, periods, participant acknowledgments). Washington gaming is ~30 tribal compacts plus commercial house-banked card rooms; only card rooms are mandatory participants in the state self-exclusion program and, per WSGC's own website, no tribal casino currently participates, so sports betting sits outside the program. The scanner's top-15 flags are almost entirely headings, statute captions and agency names (including AGA's misnomer "Washington State Gaming Commission") and are boilerplate. The genuinely material ambiguities live in the operative text the scanner under-weighted: the undefined and shifting scope of "participating tribal gaming facilities"; the two-tier forfeiture clause (participant forfeits vs. commission "may" adopt rules reaching money "obtained by an authorized gambling establishment"); the "associated ... amenities" exclusion footprint; the gap between mail-receipt enrollment and licensee notice; the reach of the RCW 9.46.071(3) immunity; the "administration of the program" data-use limit; the fee-increase bar contingent on a B&O tax subsection; the un-promulgated notarization requirement WSGC applies to mail-in forms; and the "72 hours" forwarding clock. Several are partly resolved by WAC 230-23-025/-030/-035 (not reproduced in the AGA excerpt) and by WSGC's public statements, which are cited where found.

## [medium] undefined_term — "The exclusion is in effect at all licensed house-banked card rooms and participating tribal gaming facilities in the state of Washington, which is subject to ch"

- **Why costly:** 'Participating tribal gaming facilities' is never defined in RCW 9.46.071 or WAC 230-23 (WAC 230-23-005 defines 'licensee' as only a house-banked card room and 'self-exclusion list' by reference back to the same undefined phrase). Because tribal participation is voluntary under RCW 9.46.071(2)(b) and WAC 230-23-035 mutual-sharing agreements, the geographic footprint of an exclusion changes without rulemaking. Card rooms must tell enrollees what they are signing up for; tribes evaluating a sharing agreement cannot tell whether 'participation' is all-or-nothing (casino floor plus sportsbook plus amenities) or severable; and multi-jurisdiction operators cannot map WA exclusions into their systems. Sports betting—the growth product—is effectively outside the statewide program.
- **Who bears it:** Tribal gaming enterprises negotiating sharing agreements; house-banked card room licensees (enrollment disclosures, staff training); patrons who believe they are excluded from tribal sportsbooks
- **Already resolved by:** WSGC self-exclusion page states 'Tribal casinos are not currently participating in the Washington state self-exclusion program' and directs patrons to each tribal casino's own program; WAC 230-23-035 authorizes sharing agreements but does not define participation scope https://wsgc.wa.gov/responsible-gaming-self-exclusion/self-exclusion-voluntarily-exclude-yourself-gambling-activities
- **Clarification question:** When a tribe executes a mutual sharing agreement under WAC 230-23-035, does 'participating tribal gaming facility' status attach to all gaming at that facility (including compact-authorized sports wagering and any mobile on-premises wagering) or may a tribe limit participation to specified games or venues, and how and when will the Commission publish the current list of participating facilities to licensees and enrollees?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium recommend that tribal members treat sharing-agreement participation as severable by product line (e.g., sportsbook only), and what disclosure should card rooms give enrollees about the current absence of tribal participation?

## [high] discretionary_modal — "forfeits all moneys and things of value obtained by the individual or owed to the individual by an authorized gambling establishment as a result of prohibited w"

- **Why costly:** The statute creates two distinct forfeiture pools: (i) what the participant obtained or is owed (mandatory), and (ii) what the establishment obtained from the participant, i.e., the house's win and rake (discretionary—'may adopt rules'). WAC 230-23-030 implements only pool (i): licensees confiscate the participant's money and chips, cut a check within three business days, and send a minimum of 70% to the RCW 41.05.751 problem gambling account. Whether the Commission can later, by rule or by order, require disgorgement of house revenue derived from an excluded patron is a real balance-sheet exposure for card rooms whose surveillance misses an enrollee, and it drives the business case for facial-recognition and ID-scanning investment.
- **Who bears it:** House-banked card room licensees (and participating tribes by agreement); also cage/compliance staff who must decide what to confiscate at the table
- **Already resolved by:** WAC 230-23-030 (confiscation of participant's money and things of value; 70% minimum to problem gambling account; three-business-day check; one-year retention) implements the mandatory participant-forfeiture clause but does not exercise the discretionary establishment-forfeiture authority https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=230-23-030
- **Clarification question:** Does the Commission intend to exercise its RCW 9.46.071(2)(c)(i) authority to require forfeiture of moneys 'obtained by an authorized gambling establishment' (house win, rake, tournament fees) from a registered participant, and pending any such rule, is a licensee's obligation limited to confiscation of the participant's chips, cash-on-table and unpaid winnings under WAC 230-23-030?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should members reserve or insure against house-win disgorgement for excluded-patron play, and should the Consilium seek an interpretive statement that WAC 230-23-030 is the exclusive forfeiture remedy?

## [medium] vague_qualifier — "all services and/or amenities associated with these gaming facilities including, but not limited to, restaurants, bars, bowling alleys, check cashing services, "

- **Why costly:** 'Associated with' is unbounded and the list is non-exhaustive. Many Washington house-banked card rooms are a licensed room inside a larger restaurant/bar/entertainment complex, often under separate ownership or lease. If the exclusion footprint is the whole complex, the licensee must trespass excluded persons from a restaurant it may not control, retrain non-gaming staff, extend surveillance coverage, and faces WAC 230-23-030 'immediately remove' obligations for a person merely eating dinner. If the footprint is only the licensed gaming area, none of that is required. The provision is drafted as a participant acknowledgment, but WAC 230-23-030 converts it into a licensee enforcement duty.
- **Who bears it:** House-banked card room licensees co-located with hospitality venues; landlords/affiliated restaurant operators; tribal resort operators (hotel, golf, concerts) if they participate
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found 
- **Clarification question:** For a house-banked card room located within a larger restaurant, bar or entertainment premises, does the exclusion under WAC 230-23-020(3) and the removal duty under WAC 230-23-030 extend to the entire premises or only to the licensed gambling area and amenities operated by the licensee, and does 'associated with' turn on common ownership, common entrance, or physical adjacency?
- **Docket question (gaming):** What operational standard (common-ownership test vs. licensed-floor test) should the Consilium propose so that member card rooms in mixed-use venues can define their trespass perimeter consistently?

## [medium] conflicting_cross_reference — "The enrollment period selected begins and the participant is considered enrolled: (a) Upon receipt of the form by mail; or (b) The date the completed form was a"

- **Why costly:** Enrollment—and thus the participant's forfeiture status and the exclusion's legal effect—begins on receipt by the Commission (mail) or acceptance by any licensee (in person), but other licensees learn of it only when the Commission pushes a list update (WAC 230-23-030 requires implementation 'within 48 hours' of notice). During the gap a participant may gamble at a second card room that has no notice. It is unclear whether that room must confiscate winnings retroactively, whether it has committed a violation, and whether the jackpot-verification duty in WAC 230-23-030 is measured against the list as issued or as it should have been. Tying a licensee's liability to an event it cannot observe is the core exposure.
- **Who bears it:** House-banked card room licensees other than the enrolling one; participants whose winnings may be clawed back
- **Already resolved by:** WAC 230-23-030 (licensee must implement list updates within 48 hours of Commission notice) sets the licensee-side clock but does not address the pre-notice window https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=230-23-030
- **Clarification question:** Is a licensee's obligation to exclude and confiscate under WAC 230-23-030 measured from the participant's enrollment date under WAC 230-23-015(2) or from the licensee's receipt of the Commission's list update, and does a licensee that pays a jackpot to a person not yet on its received list commit a violation or incur a forfeiture obligation?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium seek a safe-harbor amendment (liability runs from list receipt plus 48 hours) or a Commission interpretive statement, and how should members document list-receipt timestamps in the meantime?

## [medium] undefined_term — "An individual who participates in the self-exclusion program does not have a cause of action against the state of Washington, the commission, or any gambling es"

- **Why costly:** The immunity is framed as a bar on a participant's civil cause of action. It does not on its face cover (a) claims by third parties (spouses, creditors, estates), (b) claims arising from marketing to an excluded person (arguably not 'processing or enforcing'), (c) Consumer Protection Act theories pleaded as unfair practice rather than negligence, or (d) administrative discipline by the Commission for the same failure. Operators price insurance, surveillance spend and settlement posture on the assumption of broad immunity; if the immunity is narrower, that assumption is wrong. Note also that neither the statute nor WAC 230-23 states any penalty schedule for licensee non-compliance, so the disciplinary exposure is set by general RCW 9.46.075 / WAC 230-03 authority.
- **Who bears it:** House-banked card room licensees and their D&O/GL insurers; participating tribes (though tribal sovereign immunity separately applies)
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found 
- **Clarification question:** Does the Commission read RCW 9.46.071(3) as precluding disciplinary action against a licensee for a good-faith failure to detect a participant, and does the Commission regard sending targeted marketing to a participant as an 'act or omission in processing or enforcing' the program (and therefore within the immunity) or as an independent violation of WAC 230-23-030?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium advise members that RCW 9.46.071(3) is a civil-litigation shield only and that regulatory discipline and third-party/CPA claims remain live, and should it seek a published Commission enforcement-tolerance policy?

## [medium] vague_qualifier — "Any personal information collected, stored, or accessed under the self-exclusion program may only be used for the administration of the self-exclusion program a"

- **Why costly:** 'Administration of the program' is undefined. Licensees must suppress marketing (WAC 230-23-030) which in practice means loading the list into CRM/direct-mail vendor systems, sharing across commonly-owned properties, and matching against player-club databases; participating tribes must be sent the list. Each is arguably 'administration' or arguably prohibited dissemination. The provision also collides with WAC 230-23-020(6) ('disclosure of certain information is necessary'). Mis-reading it either way is costly: too narrow and the licensee violates the marketing-suppression duty; too broad and it risks a data-misuse finding and privacy claims.
- **Who bears it:** House-banked card room licensees and their marketing/CRM vendors; multi-property operators; participating tribes
- **Already resolved by:** WAC 230-23-025 permits sharing of limited identifying information (name, address, ID number, photograph, description) with contracted service providers provided the licensee does not disclose the reason or that the person is on the list, and adds 'or as otherwise permitted by law' https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=230-23-025
- **Clarification question:** Does loading participant identifiers into a third-party marketing-suppression or CRM vendor, or sharing them among commonly-owned licensed card rooms, constitute 'administration of the self-exclusion program' under RCW 9.46.071(4) and WAC 230-23-025, and what contractual or technical controls does the Commission expect a licensee to impose on such vendors?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium publish a model vendor data-sharing clause and a recommended minimum data set for suppression files so members can comply with both WAC 230-23-025 and -030?

## [low] conflicting_cross_reference — "During any period in which RCW 82.04.285(2) is in effect, the commission may not increase fees payable by licensees under its jurisdiction for the purpose of fu"

- **Why costly:** Whether the Commission can levy problem-gambling fee increases on card rooms and other licensees turns entirely on a subsection of the B&O tax code that the AGA guide does not reproduce or explain. If the 0.26% 'contests of chance' surtax dedicated to the problem gambling account lapses, the fee-increase bar lifts automatically and the Commission gains contracting and fee authority under (5)(b). Licensees budgeting fee exposure need to know the surtax status and any sunset.
- **Who bears it:** All WSGC licensees (card rooms, punchboard/pull-tab, bingo, distributors, manufacturers)
- **Already resolved by:** RCW 82.04.285(2) is currently in effect (additional tax at 0.26% of gross income from contests of chance after June 30, 2024) and the version effective January 1, 2027 retains subsection (2), so the fee-increase bar continues to apply https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=82.04.285
- **Clarification question:** Does the Commission confirm that RCW 82.04.285(2) is 'in effect' for purposes of RCW 9.46.071(5)(a) through and after the January 1, 2027 amendment to RCW 82.04.285, such that no licensee fee increase for problem-gambling funding is currently authorized?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium monitor the 2027 B&O amendment and any legislative proposal to repeal or redirect the RCW 82.04.285(2) surtax as a trigger for renewed licensee fee exposure?

## [medium] vague_qualifier — "the tribe's class III gaming compact may be amended ... to authorize the tribe to conduct and operate sports wagering on its Indian lands, provided the amendmen"

- **Why costly:** 'Addresses' sets no substantive floor. The AGA heading 'Responsible Gaming Plan Required' overstates the statute: no plan, self-exclusion mechanism, limit-setting tool, advertising standard or funding commitment is actually mandated. The content is left to compact negotiation and Interior approval, so 20+ tribes have negotiated bespoke responsible-gaming appendices, and the statewide self-exclusion program does not reach sports wagering at all (WAC 230-23-005 limits the program to card rooms and participating tribal facilities; WSGC reports no tribal participation). Tribes bear negotiation cost and uncertainty about what the Commission will treat as sufficient; commercial parties relying on the AGA heading may wrongly assume a state-level plan requirement exists.
- **Who bears it:** Federally recognized tribes negotiating or amending sports-wagering compacts; sportsbook technology/management vendors contracting with tribes
- **Already resolved by:** WSGC sports wagering requirements page reflects that responsible-gaming terms are set in the negotiated compact amendments (appendix-level provisions) rather than by statewide rule https://wsgc.wa.gov/tribal-partnerships/sports-wagering/sports-wagering-requirements-and-rules
- **Clarification question:** What minimum responsible and problem gambling provisions (e.g., self-exclusion, deposit/time limits, advertising standards, helpline signage, funding) does the Commission consider necessary for a compact amendment to 'address' responsible and problem gambling under RCW 9.46.0364(1), and does the Commission expect tribal sports wagering self-exclusion to be integrated with the statewide program?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium propose a uniform model responsible-gaming appendix for Washington sports-wagering compact amendments, and should it push for a state–tribal self-exclusion data bridge covering sportsbooks?

## [low] unbounded_deadline — "The form must be: (a) Completed with no areas left blank; and (b) Signed under penalty of perjury by the person seeking self-exclusion. (3) Upon receipt of a co"

- **Why costly:** Two related gaps. First, '72 hours' is not stated as calendar or business hours and the trigger ('receipt of a completed form') is undefined—a form missing the photo or ID is arguably not 'completed', but the licensee that holds it is the one exposed if the Commission later deems it complete. Second, the codified rule (WSR 22-05-019, eff. May 1, 2022) imposes no notarization or counselor-signature requirement for mail submissions, yet WSGC's website requires mail-in forms to be 'notarized, or ask a certified gambling counselor to sign it.' An un-promulgated completeness condition creates inconsistent enrollment dates (WAC 230-23-015(2)(a) starts enrollment 'upon receipt') and inconsistent licensee guidance to patrons. For a licensee, missing the 72-hour window is a discrete, easily auditable violation.
- **Who bears it:** House-banked card room licensees (forwarding deadline and staff guidance); participants whose mail-in enrollment may be rejected or delayed
- **Already resolved by:** WSGC website (enrollment page) states the notarization/counselor-signature practice for mail-in forms; the codified WAC 230-23-010 contains no such requirement https://wsgc.wa.gov/enrolling-self-exclusion-program
- **Clarification question:** Is the 72-hour forwarding period in WAC 230-23-010(3) measured in calendar hours from the licensee's physical receipt of a form that satisfies subsections (1)(a)(i)-(iii) and (2), and what is the legal basis for the notarization or certified-counselor signature requirement applied to mail-in forms given that WAC 230-23-010(1)(b) does not contain it—will the Commission codify it?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium ask the Commission to codify the mail-in verification requirement and define 'completed form' so members can give patrons accurate enrollment guidance and set internal forwarding SLAs?

Re: Request for Interpretive Clarification — RCW 9.46.0364, RCW 9.46.071 and Chapter 230-23 WAC (Statewide Self-Exclusion Program)

Dear Director:

We write on behalf of licensed house-banked card room operators and tribal gaming enterprises that participate in, or are evaluating participation in, the statewide self-exclusion program established under RCW 9.46.071 and chapter 230-23 WAC. Our members are committed to effective self-exclusion and seek only to ensure that their compliance programs reflect the Commission's reading of several provisions whose application is unclear on the face of the text. We respectfully request written guidance on the following questions.

1. Scope of "participating tribal gaming facilities." WAC 230-23-020(3) provides that an exclusion "is in effect at all licensed house-banked card rooms and participating tribal gaming facilities in the state of Washington, which is subject to change." Neither RCW 9.46.071 nor WAC 230-23-005 defines "participating tribal gaming facility," and the Commission's website presently states that "Tribal casinos are not currently participating in the Washington state self-exclusion program." (a) When a tribe executes a mutual sharing agreement under WAC 230-23-035, does participation attach to all gaming at the facility, including compact-authorized sports wagering, or may it be limited to specified games or venues? (b) How and how often will the Commission publish the list of participating facilities to licensees and enrollees, and what disclosure should licensees make to enrollees regarding tribal participation at the time of enrollment?

2. Forfeiture of amounts obtained by the establishment. RCW 9.46.071(2)(c)(i) provides that a participant "forfeits all moneys and things of value obtained by the individual or owed to the individual by an authorized gambling establishment," and separately that the Commission "may adopt rules for the forfeiture of any moneys or things of value, including wagers, obtained by an authorized gambling establishment while an individual is registered." WAC 230-23-030 implements confiscation of the participant's money and things of value and the distribution of confiscated funds. (a) Does the Commission intend to exercise its discretionary authority to require forfeiture of amounts obtained by the establishment (house win, rake, tournament fees) from a participant? (b) Pending any such rule, please confirm that a licensee's forfeiture obligation is limited to the confiscation and distribution steps set out in WAC 230-23-030.

3. Premises footprint of the exclusion. WAC 230-23-020(3) extends the exclusion to "all services and/or amenities associated with these gaming facilities including, but not limited to, restaurants, bars, bowling alleys, check cashing services, cash advances." For a licensed card room located within a larger restaurant, bar or entertainment premises—including premises under separate ownership or lease—does the exclusion and the removal duty in WAC 230-23-030 extend to the entire premises or only to the licensed gambling area and amenities operated by the licensee? Does "associated with" turn on common ownership, common entrance, or physical adjacency?

4. Timing of licensee obligations relative to enrollment. WAC 230-23-015(2) provides that enrollment begins "upon receipt of the form by mail" or "the date the completed form was accepted by the licensee or by us when submitted in person," while WAC 230-23-030 requires licensees to implement list updates within 48 hours of Commission notice. (a) Is a licensee's obligation to exclude, to verify jackpot winners, and to confiscate measured from the participant's enrollment date or from the licensee's receipt of the Commission's list update? (b) Does a licensee that pays a jackpot to, or accepts wagers from, a person who has enrolled but does not yet appear on the list received by that licensee commit a violation or incur a forfeiture obligation?

5. Scope of RCW 9.46.071(3). That subsection provides that a participant "does not have a cause of action against ... any gambling establishment, its employees, or officers for any acts or omissions in processing or enforcing the requirements of the self-exclusion program, including a failure to prevent an individual from gambling." (a) Does the Commission read this provision as bearing on its own disciplinary authority with respect to a licensee's good-faith failure to detect a participant? (b) Does the Commission regard the transmission of promotional material to a participant as an "act or omission in processing or enforcing" the program, or as an independent violation of the marketing-suppression duty in WAC 230-23-030? (c) Does the Commission intend to publish an enforcement or penalty framework specific to chapter 230-23 WAC?

6. "Administration of the self-exclusion program." RCW 9.46.071(4) limits use of participant information to "the administration of the self-exclusion program," and WAC 230-23-025 permits sharing of limited identifying information with contracted service providers. Please confirm whether (a) loading participant identifiers into a third-party marketing-suppression or CRM vendor's system, and (b) sharing participant identifiers among commonly-owned licensed card rooms, constitute administration of the program, and what contractual or technical safeguards the Commission expects licensees to impose on such vendors.

7. Fee-increase bar under RCW 9.46.071(5). Subsection (5)(a) bars problem-gambling fee increases "during any period in which RCW 82.04.285(2) is in effect." Please confirm the Commission's position that RCW 82.04.285(2) is in effect for this purpose, including following the amendment to RCW 82.04.285 effective January 1, 2027, and that no licensee fee increase for problem-gambling funding is presently authorized.

8. Responsible and problem gambling content of sports wagering compact amendments. RCW 9.46.0364(1) conditions a compact amendment authorizing sports wagering on the amendment's "address[ing] ... responsible and problem gambling." Please identify the minimum provisions the Commission considers necessary to satisfy this condition and state whether the Commission expects, or would support, integration of tribal sports-wagering self-exclusion with the statewide program.

9. Completed forms and the 72-hour forwarding period. WAC 230-23-010(3) requires that "upon receipt of a completed form, the licensee will forward it to us within 72 hours." (a) Is the period measured in calendar hours from the licensee's physical receipt of a form satisfying WAC 230-23-010(1)(a)(i)–(iii) and (2)? (b) The Commission's website indicates that mail-in forms must be notarized or signed by a certified gambling counselor, a requirement that does not appear in WAC 230-23-010(1)(b). Please identify the legal basis for that requirement and indicate whether the Commission intends to codify it, and confirm whether a form lacking such verification is a "completed form" for purposes of WAC 230-23-015(2)(a).

We would welcome the opportunity to meet with Commission staff to discuss these questions and to share our members' operational experience with the program. Thank you for your attention to this request.
