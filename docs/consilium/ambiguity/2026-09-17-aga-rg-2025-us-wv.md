# Guidance ambiguity review — Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide 2025 — West Virginia

> Source: https://www.americangaming.org/resources/responsible-gaming-regulations-and-statutes-guide/ (agency_guidance, US-WV, effective None). Scanned 2026-09-17: 182 textual findings; 8 judged material by the Consilium (claude-fable-5-1). Clarification request below is a DRAFT — NOT SENT; a named human must approve any submission.

The document is the West Virginia chapter of the American Gaming Association's 2025 Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide — a private-trade-association compilation of verbatim excerpts from West Virginia statutes (W. Va. Code §§29-22A, 29-22B, 29-22E) and Lottery Commission legislative rules (179 CSR 8, 9, 10). It has no independent legal force; the operative ambiguities live in the excerpted primary text. The chapter covers (i) the casino exclusion/self-exclusion regime (179 CSR 8 §§126–130: duty to exclude on a 'know or reasonably should know' standard, an affirmative duty to report patrons meeting exclusion criteria, a 'public record' exclusion list containing home addresses and photographs, a rule that all non-disruptive-conduct exclusions are 'permanent' alongside a petition-for-removal mechanism), (ii) iGaming/sports-wagering responsible-gaming page and limit-setting requirements (179 CSR 9-13.4; 179 CSR 10-5), (iii) blanket Director pre-approval of 'all advertising' by licensees, agents and affiliates for video lottery, sports pools and interactive gaming, with further requirements deferred to the MICS, and (iv) 1-800-GAMBLER signage. The scanner's top-15 hits are all artifacts of scanning a compilation ('West Virginia', 'The Commission', 'The Director', 'Statutes Guide') and are immaterial; the material exposure is in the constructive-knowledge and reporting standards, the scope and duration of self-exclusion across land-based and online channels, the public-record status of the list, and the undefined breadth and un-deadlined timing of advertising pre-approval. Research confirms: the correct citation is 179 CSR 9-21 (not '179-9-2'); the Lottery administers a statewide voluntary exclusion program that in practice extends to casino-affiliated online products with a one-year minimum before a removal petition; the Lottery issued Sports Wagering & iGaming MICS (January 2026) that may define advertising and limit requirements; and 2025 HB 3154, which would have stripped Commission authority to restrict LVL advertising/promotions, failed on the House floor 45–52, so the pre-approval regime stands unchanged.

## [medium] vague_qualifier / constructive-knowledge standard — "The casino licensee shall exclude or eject any excluded person from its premises if the casino licensee or the licensee's agents know or reasonably should know "

- **Why costly:** 'Reasonably should know' is a constructive-knowledge standard with no stated benchmark. Read narrowly, a licensee satisfies it by distributing the Commission's list to security and checking ID at the cage; read broadly, it requires facial-recognition surveillance, real-time list matching at entry and on the gaming floor, and loyalty-card blocking. The difference is a six- or seven-figure technology and staffing spend per property, and a self-excluded patron who plays undetected generates disciplinary exposure and potential wager-refund/disgorgement demands regardless of the licensee's actual knowledge.
- **Who bears it:** The five racetrack/resort casino licensees (and their security/surveillance vendors); indirectly, patrons whose wagers may be voided.
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found 
- **Clarification question:** What minimum controls (e.g., ID check at cage/loyalty enrollment, list distribution frequency, surveillance matching) does the Commission consider sufficient for a casino licensee to have satisfied the 'reasonably should know' standard in 179 CSR 8-126.2, and is a licensee that has implemented those controls deemed compliant if an excluded person nonetheless enters and plays?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium recommend that members adopt a documented 'reasonable controls' baseline (list refresh cadence, ID/loyalty matching, surveillance hits) as a safe-harbor argument, and seek Commission concurrence in writing before investing in facial-recognition or similar systems?

## [medium] undefined scope of affirmative reporting duty / discretionary trigger — "The casino licensee shall inform the Commission, in writing, of the names of persons that it knows or should know who meet the criteria for placement on an excl"

- **Why costly:** Read together with criterion 128.1(e) ('has realized that he or she has a compulsive gaming disorder and has requested in writing to be excluded'), the duty is narrow — report patrons who have submitted a written self-exclusion request. But the 'should know' language and the phrase 'meet the criteria' can be read to require licensees to report any patron who displays indicia of compulsive gambling (chasing losses, extended sessions, ATM abuse) even absent a written request. That reading converts hosts and floor staff into mandatory reporters, requires a behavioral-monitoring program, creates liability for under-reporting, and raises privacy and customer-relations risk for over-reporting.
- **Who bears it:** Casino licensees (compliance/host staff, training, monitoring systems); patrons who may be reported and listed without having requested exclusion.
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found 
- **Clarification question:** Does the reporting duty in 179 CSR 8-126.3 extend to patrons who exhibit signs of problem gambling but have not made a written self-exclusion request under 128.1(e), and if so what observable indicators or thresholds trigger the duty and within what time must the written report be made?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium take the position that 126.3 is limited to written self-exclusion requests and the other enumerated (non-RG) criteria, and how should members document 'know or should know' determinations to defend either under- or over-reporting?

## [medium] conflicting cross-reference / undefined term (public record vs. self-exclusion confidentiality) — "The Commission shall maintain a list of persons to be ejected or excluded from the casino. The exclusion list is a public record. The list may be distributed to"

- **Why costly:** Section 127 declares the entire exclusion list — which under 128.1(e) includes persons who self-identified a 'compulsive gaming disorder' — a public record containing name, DOB, aliases, photograph, occupation and home address. If literally applied, a FOIA request could expose health-adjacent information about self-excluders, creating tort/privacy exposure for the Commission and chilling program enrollment; if operators treat the list as confidential (as Lottery program materials imply), they must build access controls, vendor NDAs, and data-handling procedures that the rule text does not require and may face a FOIA-type demand they cannot lawfully refuse. Operators also cannot tell whether sharing the list with affiliated online partners, MSPs, or out-of-state affiliates is permitted, required, or a disclosure violation.
- **Who bears it:** Self-excluded patrons (privacy); casino licensees, MSPs and sportsbook/iGaming partners (data-handling controls, list-sharing legal risk); the Commission (FOIA exposure).
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found 
- **Clarification question:** Does the 'public record' designation in 179 CSR 8-127.1 apply to persons placed on the list under the voluntary self-exclusion criterion in 128.1(e), and may (or must) a casino licensee share those entries with its contracted sports pool, interactive gaming and management-services partners for account blocking?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium seek a formal Commission position (or rule amendment) distinguishing the involuntary exclusion list (public) from the voluntary self-exclusion list (confidential, shareable only with licensees/MSPs for blocking), and what interim data-handling protocol should members adopt?

## [medium] conflicting cross-reference (permanent exclusion vs. §130 petition for removal) — "A temporary exclusion shall apply only to a person excluded or ejected for disruptive conduct. All other exclusions shall be permanent."

- **Why costly:** §129.5 makes every self-exclusion 'permanent' while §130 lets any listed person petition for removal, and §129.4 states a name 'shall remain' if no hearing is requested. Operators cannot determine from the rule whether a self-excluded patron who later petitions can ever be reinstated, whether there is a minimum term before petition, or whether reinstatement extends to affiliated online accounts. Reinstating a patron the Commission still regards as permanently excluded is a disciplinary violation and exposes the operator to claims by the patron; refusing to reinstate a lawfully removed patron is a customer-relations and potential discrimination issue. Lottery program practice (indefinite exclusion, one-year minimum before removal request) is not in the rule text.
- **Who bears it:** Casino licensees and online operators (reinstatement procedures, account-reactivation controls); self-excluded patrons (uncertain path to removal).
- **Already resolved by:** Partially — West Virginia Lottery Self-Exclusion Program page (program is indefinite until the person requests removal; minimum one year before removal petition) and Lottery Self-Exclusion Removal Application form; not reflected in the rule text. https://wvlottery.com/iplay-with-purpose/self-exclusion
- **Clarification question:** For a person placed on the list under 128.1(e), what is the minimum period before a §130 removal petition may be granted, what standard governs the Director's/Commission's removal decision, and how and when are licensees and their online partners notified that a name has been removed so that accounts may be reactivated?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium recommend that members treat all 128.1(e) exclusions as indefinite with reactivation only upon written Commission notice of removal, and press for a rule amendment reconciling §129.5 with §130 and codifying the one-year minimum?

## [high] undefined scope / unbounded channel coverage — "The person has realized that he or she has a compulsive gaming disorder and has requested in writing to be excluded from the casino and/or all of the state's fo"

- **Why costly:** The criterion is drafted for land-based casinos and 'four pari-mutuel racetracks' (the Greenbrier resort casino and all mobile sports-wagering and interactive-gaming platforms are not mentioned), and 'and/or' allows venue-by-venue exclusion. Operators must decide whether a casino self-exclusion automatically bars the patron's online sportsbook/iGaming accounts run by that casino's MSP partners, other casinos' online skins, and limited video lottery retailers. Accepting online wagers from a person on the casino list is the single most common exclusion enforcement scenario nationally and carries fines, wager disgorgement and licensing consequences; over-blocking (excluding a patron from platforms he did not select) creates customer disputes and revenue loss. Building cross-channel list synchronization across five casinos and roughly a dozen online skins is a material IT/compliance project.
- **Who bears it:** Casino licensees, sports pool intermediaries, interactive gaming operators and MSPs (list-synchronization systems, KYC/exclusion screening); patrons.
- **Already resolved by:** Partially — West Virginia Lottery program description states voluntary exclusion applies to all four racetrack casinos and the Greenbrier and to the mobile applications and online gaming associated with them; 179 CSR 9 requires intermediaries to verify patrons are not self-excluded. The rule text itself remains channel-limited and does not address limited video lottery retailers or partial ('and/or') elections. https://wvlottery.com/iplay-with-purpose/self-exclusion
- **Clarification question:** Does a written self-exclusion request under 179 CSR 8-128.1(e) automatically extend to (a) all mobile sports wagering and interactive gaming accounts operated under any West Virginia casino license, (b) the Greenbrier, and (c) limited video lottery retailers, or may the patron elect a subset; and within what time after Commission notice must each operator and MSP block the affected accounts?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium recommend members adopt a default 'all channels, all licensees' block on any Commission-listed name pending clarification, and what timing standard (e.g., 24 hours from Lottery notice) should be proposed to the Commission as the industry benchmark?

## [high] undefined term ('all advertising', 'affiliates') / unbounded deadline (no approval turnaround) / miscited cross-reference (shown as '179-9-2'; actual section is 179 CSR 9-21) — "The Lottery Director shall approve all advertising in advance of a casino licensee, its sports pool intermediaries, its agents, or its affiliates publishing or "

- **Why costly:** Neither 179 CSR 9-21 nor 179 CSR 10-22 defines 'advertising' or 'affiliates', and neither sets a review deadline or deemed-approval period. Read literally, every social-media post, push notification, e-mail, odds boost, bonus offer, affiliate review site, and influencer post by any 'affiliate' requires prior Director sign-off — an operational impossibility for real-time sports promotions that forces operators either to run unapproved creative (enforcement exposure) or to withhold time-sensitive promotions (competitive loss). Operators also cannot tell whether registered marketing affiliates' content is the operator's responsibility to submit, and the 'further requirements' are deferred to MICS that are not incorporated in the rule text. The Guide's citation to '179-9-2' does not exist as an advertising section and will misdirect compliance teams.
- **Who bears it:** Casino licensees, sports pool intermediaries, interactive gaming operators, MSPs and registered marketing affiliates (marketing operations, legal review, delayed campaigns); ultimately the Lottery (review backlog).
- **Already resolved by:** Partially — the correct citation is 179 CSR 9-21 (Justia/Cornell), and the Lottery has issued Sports Wagering & iGaming MICS (January 2026 edition) and an affiliate registrant program that may specify submission mechanics; the rule text still contains no definition of 'advertising' or 'affiliate' and no turnaround deadline. https://assets.ctfassets.net/nm98451qj5dg/kmK8dIROgjVQA8qcewE67/7c27e5832b9e5d0f60ec33ac2d2a722b/MICS_Sports_and_I-Gaming_JANUARY_2026.pdf
- **Clarification question:** For purposes of 179 CSR 9-21.1 and 179 CSR 10-22.1, (a) which categories of communication constitute 'advertising' requiring prior approval (e.g., organic social posts, push/e-mail to existing account holders, in-app odds boosts, affiliate-generated content), (b) does 'affiliates' mean corporate affiliates, registered marketing affiliates, or both, (c) what is the Director's review period and is there a deemed-approval or template/pre-cleared-category mechanism, and (d) which MICS provisions currently implement the 'further requirements'?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium propose to the Commission a tiered approval framework (pre-cleared templates and categories, expedited review for time-sensitive promotions, operator responsibility limited to affiliate content it controls), and in the interim should members adopt a uniform submission protocol to demonstrate good faith?

## [medium] undefined term ('promotional activities') / discretionary approval without standard or deadline — "Conduct no video lottery advertising and promotional activities without the prior written approval of the director."

- **Why costly:** 'Promotional activities' is undefined in §29-22A-9(a)(12) and may reach player loyalty programs, comps, free play, tournaments, and third-party sponsorships at racetrack video lottery facilities. A broad reading requires written Director approval before any comp or loyalty change, which is administratively heavy and creates strict-liability exposure for routine marketing; a narrow reading risks a finding that an unapproved loyalty promotion was conducted 'without prior written approval'. The 2025 Legislature considered and rejected (HB 3154, failed 45–52) a bill that would have stripped the Commission's authority to restrict LVL advertising and promotions, so the regime stands and legislative relief is not imminent.
- **Who bears it:** Racetrack video lottery licensees and limited video lottery operators/retailers (marketing and loyalty-program administration; enforcement exposure).
- **Already resolved by:** Legislative status resolved — 2025 HB 3154 (would have removed Commission authority to restrict LVL advertising/promotional activities) failed on the House floor 45–52 on April 1, 2025; the statutory pre-approval requirement remains in force. The definitional ambiguity itself is unresolved. https://wvmetronews.com/2025/04/01/house-rejects-bill-allowing-video-lottery-retailers-to-advertise/
- **Clarification question:** Does 'promotional activities' in W. Va. Code §29-22A-9(a)(12) include player loyalty/rewards programs, complimentaries, free play, and tournaments conducted by a racetrack video lottery licensee, and if so may such programs be approved on an annual program basis rather than per-promotion?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium seek an interpretive letter or MICS amendment carving loyalty/comp programs out of 'promotional activities' or permitting annual program-level approvals, and how should members document approvals in the meantime?

## [medium] undefined term (types and mechanics of 'responsible gaming limits') / unbounded implementation standard — "Rules governing self-imposed responsible gaming limits and the ability for the patron to establish those limits."

- **Why costly:** 179 CSR 9-13.4.4 and 179 CSR 10-5.15.4 require operators to publish and offer 'self-imposed responsible gaming limits' but do not specify which limits (deposit, wager, loss, time, session), the available periods, whether increases are subject to a cooling-off period, whether limits are per-account or per-patron across skins, or whether decreases must take effect immediately. Each choice is a product-build decision for every platform and a divergence from the multi-state norm (24-hour or 72-hour cooling-off on increases) can be characterized by the Lottery or plaintiffs as inadequate. Operators also cannot determine whether the narrative in the Guide ('ensuring players can set both wagering and time limits') reflects a binding requirement or a summary.
- **Who bears it:** Sports pool operators, interactive gaming operators and MSPs (platform development, certification, T&C drafting); patrons.
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found 
- **Clarification question:** Which limit types (deposit, wager, loss, time/session), periods, and cooling-off rules for limit increases does the Lottery require or expect under 179 CSR 9-13.4.4 and 179 CSR 10-5.15.4, and are these specified in the current Sports Wagering & iGaming MICS such that compliance with the MICS is compliance with the rule?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium recommend a common member baseline (deposit, wager, loss and time limits; daily/weekly/monthly periods; immediate decreases; 24-hour minimum cooling-off on increases) and present it to the Lottery as a proposed MICS standard?

We recognize that the Guide is a private compilation and that the operative text is the Code and Code of State Rules. The questions below concern the primary text.

1. Constructive-knowledge standard for exclusion (179 CSR 8-126.2)

The rule provides: "The casino licensee shall exclude or eject any excluded person from its premises if the casino licensee or the licensee's agents know or reasonably should know that the person is on the Commission's exclusion list."

Question 1(a): What minimum controls does the Commission consider sufficient for a casino licensee to have satisfied the "reasonably should know" standard (for example, distribution of the current list to security and cage personnel, identity verification at loyalty enrollment and at the cage, and surveillance matching at a stated cadence)?
Question 1(b): Where a licensee has implemented those controls and an excluded person nonetheless enters and wagers, does the Commission regard the licensee as having complied with §126.2?

2. Scope of the affirmative reporting duty (179 CSR 8-126.3 and 8-128.1(e))

Section 126.3 provides: "The casino licensee shall inform the Commission, in writing, of the names of persons that it knows or should know who meet the criteria for placement on an exclusion list and the reason the person meets the exclusion criteria." Criterion 128.1(e) applies where "[t]he person has realized that he or she has a compulsive gaming disorder and has requested in writing to be excluded from the casino and/or all of the state's four pari-mutuel racetracks."

Question 2(a): Is the reporting duty in §126.3, as it relates to criterion 128.1(e), limited to persons who have submitted a written self-exclusion request, or does it extend to patrons who display indicia of compulsive gambling but have made no such request?
Question 2(b): If the duty extends beyond written requests, what indicators or thresholds trigger it, and within what period must the written report be made?

3. Public-record status of the exclusion list and permitted sharing (179 CSR 8-127.1)

Section 127.1 provides: "The exclusion list is a public record. The list may be distributed to law enforcement agencies," and requires inclusion of, among other things, "[t]he person's occupation and current home and business addresses" and a photograph.

Question 3(a): Does the public-record designation apply to persons placed on the list under the voluntary self-exclusion criterion in §128.1(e)?
Question 3(b): May, or must, a casino licensee share entries relating to §128.1(e) persons with its contracted sports pool intermediaries, interactive gaming operators and management services providers for the purpose of blocking accounts, and are any restrictions placed on onward use or retention of that information?

4. Duration of self-exclusion and removal (179 CSR 8-129.5 and 8-130)

Section 129.5 provides: "A temporary exclusion shall apply only to a person excluded or ejected for disruptive conduct. All other exclusions shall be permanent." Section 130.1 provides that "[a] person who has been placed on any exclusion list may petition the Commission, in writing, and request that his or her name be removed from the exclusion list." We understand the Lottery's program materials describe voluntary exclusion as indefinite with a one-year minimum before a removal request.

Question 4(a): For a person listed under §128.1(e), what is the minimum period before a §130 petition may be granted, and what standard governs the decision?
Question 4(b): How and when are casino licensees and their online partners notified that a name has been removed, and may an operator reactivate the patron's accounts only upon that written notice?

5. Channel scope of self-exclusion (179 CSR 8-128.1(e))

Criterion 128.1(e) refers to exclusion "from the casino and/or all of the state's four pari-mutuel racetracks."

Question 5(a): Does a written self-exclusion request automatically extend to (i) all mobile sports wagering and interactive gaming accounts operated under any West Virginia casino license, (ii) The Greenbrier, and (iii) limited video lottery retailers, or may the patron elect a subset under the "and/or" language?
Question 5(b): Within what period after Commission notice must each casino licensee, sports pool intermediary, interactive gaming operator and management services provider block the affected accounts?

6. Advertising pre-approval (179 CSR 9-21.1; 179 CSR 10-22.1; W. Va. Code §29-22A-9(a)(12))

Section 9-21.1 provides: "The Lottery Director shall approve all advertising in advance of a casino licensee, its sports pool intermediaries, its agents, or its affiliates publishing or otherwise releasing such advertisements. Further requirements shall be defined by the Lottery Commission in its MICS." Section 10-22.1 is to the same effect for interactive gaming. Section 29-22A-9(a)(12) requires video lottery licensees to "[c]onduct no video lottery advertising and promotional activities without the prior written approval of the director." We note that the Guide cites the sports-wagering provision as "179-9-2"; we understand the correct citation to be 179 CSR 9-21 and would appreciate confirmation.

Question 6(b): Does "affiliates" in those sections mean corporate affiliates of the licensee, registered marketing affiliates, or both, and is the licensee responsible for submitting affiliate-generated content?
Question 6(c): What is the Director's review period, and does the Commission recognize any deemed-approval, template, or pre-cleared-category mechanism for time-sensitive promotions?
Question 6(d): Which provisions of the current Sports Wagering and iGaming MICS implement the "further requirements" referenced in §9-21.1 and §10-22.2?
Question 6(e): Does "promotional activities" in §29-22A-9(a)(12) include player loyalty or rewards programs, complimentaries, free play and tournaments, and if so may such programs be approved on an annual program basis rather than per promotion?

7. Self-imposed responsible gaming limits (179 CSR 9-13.4.4; 179 CSR 10-5.15.4)

The rules require the responsible gaming page to contain "[r]ules governing self-imposed responsible gaming limits and the ability for the patron to establish those limits."

Question 7(b): Are those specifications set out in the current MICS such that conformance with the MICS constitutes compliance with §§9-13.4.4 and 10-5.15.4?
