# Guidance ambiguity review — Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide 2025 — Virginia

> Source: https://www.americangaming.org/resources/responsible-gaming-regulations-and-statutes-guide/ (agency_guidance, US-VA, effective None). Scanned 2026-09-16: 149 textual findings; 7 judged material by the Consilium (claude-fable-5-1). Clarification request below is a DRAFT — NOT SENT; a named human must approve any submission.

The Virginia chapter of the American Gaming Association's Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide (2025) is a secondary compilation, not primary authority. It reproduces (i) 11 VAC 5-80-80 (sports betting permit holders' corporate responsible gambling policy), (ii) 11 VAC 5-90-100(J)(5) (casino facility responsible gaming plan: 60-day pre-opening submission, amendments before implementation, annual report), (iii) two parallel voluntary exclusion statutes — Va. Code § 58.1-4015.1 (lottery/sports betting article) and § 58.1-4103 (casino chapter) — with near-identical text but different recipient lists and cross-references, and (iv) 11 VAC 5-60-20 (self-exclusion request mechanics; 2/5-year/lifetime terms). Almost all 149 scanner hits are headings, citation fragments ("Code Ann", "Statutes Guide") or standard statutory shorthand ("The Board", "The Commonwealth") that a reader of the primary source would not find ambiguous. The genuinely material ambiguities sit in the operative text: the "reasonable attempts as determined by the Board" direct-marketing suppression standard (only partly narrowed by 11 VAC 5-60-40, which still lacks a suppression deadline and does not address affiliates/digital channels); the duplicative and non-identical exclusion statutes; the open-ended casino plan requirements ("any other element required by the department", amendment submission with no approval standard, undated annual report); undefined retraining frequency; and the scope of permitted intra-enterprise sharing of self-excluded names against the statutory confidentiality mandate. A background structural issue — which agency is "the Board"/"the department" — remains live: the 2025 SB1287 and 2026 HB271 Virginia Gaming Commission bills did not pass (HB271 tabled to 2027), so the Virginia Lottery Board/Virginia Lottery remain the regulator as of September 2026.

## [high] vague_qualifier / undefined_term — "Sales agents and permit holders shall make all reasonable attempts as determined by the Board to cease all direct marketing efforts to a person participating in"

- **Why costly:** 'Reasonable attempts' and 'direct marketing' set the compliance floor for CRM suppression across mail, telemarketing, email, SMS, push notifications, in-app offers, affiliate/lead-gen traffic and loyalty-club communications. 11 VAC 5-60-40 narrows this to 'targeted mailings, telemarketing promotions, player club materials, or other targeted promotional materials' but (a) qualifies the duty 'to the greatest extent practicable', (b) sets no deadline to suppress after the department adds a name (contrast the 7-day deletion deadline on removal in 11 VAC 5-60-50), and (c) is silent on affiliates and programmatic digital advertising. Every marketing-to-self-excluded-patron enforcement action in other states has turned on exactly these gaps; operators must decide whether to build one-day or seven-day suppression SLAs and whether to push suppression lists to third-party marketers.
- **Who bears it:** Sports betting permit holders, casino facility operators, lottery sales agents; their affiliate marketers and platform providers by contract
- **Already resolved by:** Partially — 11 VAC 5-60-40 (eff. Sept. 2, 2022) enumerates covered promotional channels and 11 VAC 5-60-50 sets a 7-day deletion window on removal, but no addition-side deadline, affiliate rule, or digital-channel definition exists https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title11/agency5/chapter60/section40/
- **Clarification question:** Within how many days of the department's notice adding a name to the self-exclusion list must a permit holder or casino operator suppress that individual from all targeted marketing, and does 'direct marketing' / 'targeted promotional materials' include push notifications, in-app bonus offers, SMS, retargeted digital advertising and communications sent by affiliates or third-party marketing partners on the operator's behalf?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should members adopt a uniform 24-hour suppression SLA and contractual flow-down to affiliates for Virginia now, or wait for the Lottery to define the addition-side deadline given the 7-day deletion window already exists?

## [medium] conflicting_reference — "Statute: Va. Code Ann. § 58.1-4015.1. Voluntary Exclusion Program ... Statute: Va. Code Ann. § 58.1-4103. Voluntary Exclusion Program"

- **Why costly:** Two parallel statutes impose the same program with different recipient lists: § 58.1-4015.1 limits dissemination to 'sales agents and permit holders, as defined in § 58.1-4030' while § 58.1-4103 limits it to 'lottery sales agents ... [and] owners and operators of casino gaming establishments'. Read literally, a sports betting permit holder is not an authorized recipient of the casino-chapter list and a casino is not an authorized recipient of the sports betting-article list, yet both are expected to enforce exclusion. § 58.1-4103 also defines sports betting as 'such activity is regulated by the Board' rather than by cross-reference to § 58.1-4030, and mis-cites the FOIA exemption as '§ 5.2-3700' (should be § 2.2-3700). Operators handling the list need certainty that receipt and cross-vertical use are authorized under both statutes, and that the FOIA exemption is enforceable for casino-chapter data.
- **Who bears it:** Casino operators that also hold sports betting permits (all three Virginia casinos) and their affiliated online sportsbooks; compliance/privacy counsel
- **Already resolved by:** Operationally — 11 VAC 5-60 (Self-Exclusion Program) administers a single department list and 11 VAC 5-60-40 imposes duties jointly on 'sports betting permit holder and casino gaming establishment'; the statutory text itself has not been harmonized https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title11/agency5/chapter60/
- **Clarification question:** Does the department maintain a single voluntary exclusion list under both § 58.1-4015.1 and § 58.1-4103, and confirm that a sports betting permit holder and a casino gaming establishment are each authorized recipients of that single list for purposes of both statutes, notwithstanding the differing recipient definitions in subsection B(4) of each?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium seek a technical corrections bill to consolidate the two exclusion statutes (and fix the § 5.2-3700 cite), or accept the regulatory harmonization in 11 VAC 5-60 as sufficient?

## [medium] undefined_term / discretionary_modal — "In addition, a permit holder may share the names of individuals who self-exclude across its corporate enterprise, including sharing such information with any of"

- **Why costly:** 'Corporate enterprise' and 'affiliates' are undefined. Multi-state operators want to use Virginia self-exclusion data to block the same individual on sister-brand apps in other states (a recognized best practice), but the same statute makes the list 'confidential' and limits Department dissemination. Whether the permissive 'may share' extends to out-of-state affiliates, platform/technology vendors, joint-venture partners, or a market-access partner's parent determines system design and privacy risk; a wrong reading is either a confidentiality breach (list is Privacy Act-sensitive, includes SSN) or a missed cross-state exclusion that becomes a duty-of-care claim.
- **Who bears it:** Multi-state sports betting and casino operators; platform providers and market-access partners
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found 
- **Clarification question:** May a permit holder or casino operator transmit the name (and what data elements — name only, or DOB/address/SSN) of a Virginia self-excluded individual to affiliates operating in other jurisdictions and to its platform/technology vendors for the sole purpose of enforcing exclusion, and does 'affiliate' include a market-access partner's parent or a contracted third-party operator?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should members adopt a name-plus-DOB-only cross-enterprise sharing protocol for Virginia data pending guidance, and should the Consilium request an explicit safe harbor for cross-jurisdictional exclusion sharing?

## [medium] unbounded_obligation / undefined_deadline — "11. Any other element required by the department. ... d. A facility operator shall submit any amendments to its responsible gaming plan to the department prior "

- **Why costly:** Three open ends for casino operators: (i) 'any other element required by the department' allows plan requirements to be added without rulemaking; (ii) 'submit ... prior to implementation' does not say whether the department must approve, how long it has, or whether an operator may implement on submission (a de facto approval requirement would freeze routine plan changes such as adding a treatment provider); (iii) the annual report has no due date, format, or content standard, so operators cannot calendar it and face a 'late filing' finding based on an unstated expectation.
- **Who bears it:** Casino facility operators (Bristol, Danville, Portsmouth, Norfolk, and any future Petersburg licensee)
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title11/agency5/chapter90/section100/
- **Clarification question:** (a) Is department approval required before an amended responsible gaming plan may be implemented, and if so within what review period is the department deemed to have approved; (b) what is the annual report due date and required content; and (c) will 'any other element required by the department' be communicated by written notice with a compliance period?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should members request a deemed-approval period (e.g., 30 days) for plan amendments and a fixed annual report date, or treat these as informal expectations to be managed through relationship with Lottery staff?

## [low] vague_qualifier — "e. All staff are trained upon hiring and retrained regularly;"

- **Why costly:** Retraining frequency drives recurring training spend for large retail and online workforces and is the item most easily audited. Neighboring jurisdictions specify annual (or in some cases biennial) refreshers; a permit holder that chooses biennial to save cost risks a finding that 'regularly' meant annual. The October 22, 2025 amendment to 11 VAC 5-80-80 retained the term without defining it.
- **Who bears it:** Sports betting permit holders (and casino operators by analogy under 11 VAC 5-90-100(J)(5)(b)(5))
- **Already resolved by:** nothing found https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title11/agency5/chapter80/section80/
- **Clarification question:** Will the department treat annual refresher training for all customer-facing and marketing staff as satisfying 'retrained regularly' under 11 VAC 5-80-80(C)(6)(e), and must retraining records be retained for a specified period?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium recommend an industry-wide annual-refresher baseline for Virginia so that 'regularly' is never litigated against a member?

## [low] discretionary_modal / regulatory_delegation — "Except as provided by regulation of the Board, a person who participates in the voluntary exclusion program may not petition the Board for removal from the prog"

- **Why costly:** Operators need to know whether removal is automatic at term end (so a returning patron can be re-onboarded) or requires a petition, and whether lifetime is truly irrevocable. Reinstating too early is a self-exclusion breach; refusing a validly removed patron is a customer-service and discrimination complaint. 11 VAC 5-60-50 resolves the mechanics: removal is automatic on expiration, the department notifies operators, and operators must delete the name within seven days.
- **Who bears it:** Sports betting permit holders and casino operators (account reactivation and KYC teams)
- **Already resolved by:** 11 VAC 5-60-50 (eff. Sept. 2, 2022): automatic removal on expiration; operators delete within 7 days of department notice https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title11/agency5/chapter60/section50/
- **Clarification question:** Confirm that an individual whose two- or five-year term has expired is removed automatically without a petition, that operators may reactivate accounts only upon receipt of department notice (not on the calendar expiry date), and that no regulation currently permits early removal from a lifetime exclusion.
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should member reactivation policies key exclusively off department notice rather than the patron's stated expiry date, and should the Consilium recommend a cooling-off contact protocol at reinstatement?

## [low] undefined_term — "A. The Board shall adopt regulations to establish and implement a voluntary exclusion program."

- **Why costly:** 'The Board' and 'the Department' are the Virginia Lottery Board and Virginia Lottery under current law, but bills in 2025 (SB1287) and 2026 (HB271) would have transferred casino, sports betting, fantasy and horse racing oversight to a new Virginia Gaming Commission, with the competing SB609 approach keeping the Lottery. HB271 was tabled to 2027 on March 9, 2026. Every plan filing, list-receipt protocol and marketing-suppression standard 'as determined by the Board' would migrate to a new body with power to re-set the standard; operators budgeting 2027 compliance need to know the counterparty.
- **Who bears it:** All Virginia licensees; compliance and government-affairs functions
- **Already resolved by:** Status confirmed by VPM reporting (Mar. 9, 2026): no consolidation bill enacted; Virginia Lottery Board/Virginia Lottery remain the regulator; issue carried to the 2027 session https://www.vpm.org/general-assembly/2026-03-09/gambling-bill-igaming-skill-games-virginia-lottery-krizek-lucas-frazier/
- **Clarification question:** If regulatory authority is transferred to a Virginia Gaming Commission, will existing responsible gaming plan approvals, self-exclusion list access credentials and 'reasonable attempts' determinations carry over without re-filing, and what transition period would apply?
- **Docket question (gaming):** Should the Consilium take a position on the 2027 consolidation bills and seek a grandfathering/transition clause for existing RG plan approvals and self-exclusion protocols?

Dear Director:

We write on behalf of licensed sports betting permit holders and casino gaming operators in the Commonwealth to request written clarification of several provisions governing voluntary exclusion and responsible gaming. Our members are committed to full compliance and seek this guidance so that operational systems, training programs and filings can be built to the Department's actual expectations. The questions below are presented neutrally and quote the operative text.

1. Direct marketing suppression — standard and timing.
Va. Code § 58.1-4015.1(B)(5) and § 58.1-4103(B)(5) provide that operators "shall make all reasonable attempts as determined by the Board to cease all direct marketing efforts to a person participating in the program." 11 VAC 5-60-40 requires operators to "[e]nsure that self-excluded individuals do not receive ... targeted mailings, telemarketing promotions, player club materials, or other targeted promotional materials," while 11 VAC 5-60-50 provides a seven-day period to delete names following a removal notice.
(a) Within what period after the Department's notice of an addition to the self-exclusion list does the Department expect suppression of the individual from targeted marketing to be complete?
(b) Does "direct marketing" / "other targeted promotional materials" encompass push notifications, in-application bonus offers, SMS, email, and retargeted digital advertising?
(c) Are communications sent by affiliates, lead-generation partners or other third parties on the operator's behalf attributed to the operator for this purpose, and will the Department publish or confirm the "reasonable attempts" it has "determined"?

2. Parallel exclusion statutes — single list and authorized recipients.
Section 58.1-4015.1(B)(4) limits dissemination of the list to "sales agents and permit holders, as defined in § 58.1-4030," whereas § 58.1-4103(B)(4) limits dissemination to "lottery sales agents licensed under Chapter 40 ... owners and operators of casino gaming establishments." Section 58.1-4103(B)(4) also cites the Freedom of Information Act as "§ 5.2-3700 et seq."
(a) Please confirm that the Department maintains a single voluntary exclusion list administered under 11 VAC 5-60 that satisfies both statutes.
(b) Please confirm that a sports betting permit holder and a casino gaming establishment are each authorized recipients of that list for purposes of both statutes, and that each may use the list to enforce exclusion across all gaming verticals it operates.
(c) Please confirm the Department's view that the reference to "§ 5.2-3700" is intended to refer to § 2.2-3700 and that the FOIA exemption applies to all list data.

3. Intra-enterprise sharing of self-excluded names.
Both statutes provide that an operator "may share the names of individuals who self-exclude across its corporate enterprise, including sharing such information with any of its affiliates," while also providing that the list and participants' personal information "shall be confidential."
(a) Does "corporate enterprise" / "affiliates" include entities under common ownership operating in other jurisdictions, market-access or skin partners, and contracted platform or technology providers?
(b) Which data elements may be shared for this purpose — name only, or name together with date of birth, address or other identifiers necessary to effect a match?
(c) Does the Department object to use of Virginia self-exclusion data by an affiliate to exclude the same individual from that affiliate's offerings in another state?

4. Casino responsible gaming plan — amendments, annual report, additional elements.
11 VAC 5-90-100(J)(5) provides that a facility operator "shall submit any amendments to its responsible gaming plan to the department prior to implementation," "shall submit to the department an annual report describing the facility's responsible gaming plan," and that the plan shall include "[a]ny other element required by the department."
(a) Is Department approval required before an amended plan may be implemented? If so, what is the review period, and may the operator implement if no response is received within that period?
(b) What is the due date for the annual report, and does the Department prescribe a form or minimum content?
(c) Will additional elements required under paragraph (b)(11) be communicated in writing with a stated compliance period?

5. Staff retraining frequency.
11 VAC 5-80-80(C)(6)(e), as amended effective October 22, 2025, provides that "[a]ll staff are trained upon hiring and retrained regularly." Will the Department treat annual refresher training for customer-facing, marketing and compliance personnel as satisfying this requirement, and is there a required retention period for training records?

6. Removal from the list.
Section 58.1-4015.1(B)(3) provides that, "[e]xcept as provided by regulation of the Board," a participant "may not petition the Board for removal from the program for the duration of his exclusion period." 11 VAC 5-60-50 provides for removal by the Department upon expiration and deletion by operators within seven days of notice. Please confirm that (a) removal at the end of a two- or five-year term is automatic and requires no petition; (b) operators should reactivate accounts only upon receipt of Department notice, not on the calendar expiry date; and (c) no current regulation permits early removal from a lifetime exclusion.

7. Regulatory transition.
In light of the consolidation proposals considered in the 2025 and 2026 Sessions of the General Assembly, please advise whether the Department anticipates that existing responsible gaming plan submissions, self-exclusion list access arrangements and any determinations under the provisions cited above would carry forward without re-filing in the event of a transfer of regulatory authority.

We would welcome the opportunity to meet with Department staff to discuss these questions and to provide any operational detail that would assist. Thank you for your consideration.
